Start here

Nexus at a glance

Nexus is the record system for recovery centers. Care, billing and leadership work from one record of each person, instead of three tools that drift apart. The brand has one job: make that record feel alive, careful and easy to read.

How it should feel

  • Grown, not manufactured. Carved shapes, pressed ink, warm linen. Nothing glossy.
  • A friend who is good at their job. Warm in tone, exact in detail.
  • Brief. A screen says what matters in one look. So does a sentence.
  • Calm under load. Dense tables stay quiet; colour is saved for what needs attention.
  • A little odd, on purpose. Cards shaped like leaves. A sun where a spinner would be.

What it is not

  • Hospital blue, clinical white, stock stethoscopes.
  • A wellness brand: no lotus, no gradients, no "healing journey".
  • A generic dashboard kit with a green coat of paint.
  • Rustic or twee. The woodcut is the accent, not the wallpaper.
  • Decorated data. Nothing louder than a faint leaf vein sits behind a number someone has to read.

The idea in one line

Growth, on the record.

A tree keeps its own chart. Every year it lays down a ring: wide in a good season, narrow in a hard one, and none of it is erased. Recovery is recorded the same way, one session, one note, one signed plan at a time. Nexus is where those rings are kept.

This line is a proposal for internal use and the cover of sales material. It is not yet used in the product.

Identity

The symbol is a cross-section

Cut through a trunk and you see everything that happened to it, in order, around one centre. That is also a fair description of a good clinical record. The symbol is called Convergence, and each part of it stands for something in the product.

{{svg logo/masters/nexus-symbol-convergence.svg class="sym"}}
The ringsEach one is time on the record: a session, a signed note, a posted payment. They are uneven because real progress is.
The barkThe one line that is not a ring. It wanders, the way bark keys into wood, and it holds every ring inside it. It stands for the organisation: permissions, audit, tenancy.
The pithThe person in care. Everything else in Nexus grows outward from one client record.
The three checksCare, billing and oversight. Three lines of work that meet at the centre. Where they meet is the nexus.

Carved, then pressed

The symbol is drawn the way a linocut is made: a solid block of ink with the lines cut out of it. Edges wobble slightly and every corner is rounded by the gouge. Keep that quality. Do not redraw it with perfect circles.

One colour, always

It works in a single flat ink with no gradient, outline or shadow. If a placement seems to need an effect, the placement is wrong.

Never a wheel

The three checks are unequal in length and spacing so they read as natural splits in wood, and the bark line is uneven on purpose. Made regular, it would become a car badge.

The balance

Roughly how much of any screen or printed piece each colour covers.

Linen, Birch, Oat · about 60% Pine · 20% Greens

Clay is the last few percent: the focus ring, a count that needs attention, the sun in an illustration. If Clay is the first thing you see on a screen, there is too much of it.

Rules that keep it Nexus

  • The page is Linen. Pure white appears nowhere.
  • Text is Heartwood, never black and never Pine. Pine is for the brand and for actions.
  • Brand green is not a status. "Signed" and "Ready" use the success colour with a word and an icon.
  • Pollen, Bark and Creek belong to illustration and charts. The one exception is a visited link, which is Bark.
  • No gradients and no tints made by lowering opacity over photos.

Three families, each with one job. Fraunces gives headings the warmth of pressed wood type. Source Sans 3 does the reading. Manrope sets the numbers that have to land.

Fraunces, set soft

Fraunces has a Soft axis that rounds every terminal, the same thing the press does to the logo. Nexus always uses it at Soft 100, with the Wonk alternates off. The bundled files are cut that way, so there is nothing to set.

Three jobs, no overlap

FamilyYesNever
FrauncesOne heading per panel; titles; quotes.Buttons, labels, tables, numbers.
Source Sans 3Everything that is read or typed.Page titles.
ManropeFigures that stand alone, with a label.Sentences, or numbers inside a sentence.

Rules

  • Sentence case everywhere. Capitals only for the small label style.
  • No all-capitals headings, and no heading tracked tighter than −0.015em.
  • If everything is Fraunces, nothing is. One Fraunces heading per panel.

Delivery

All three are open-licensed (SIL Open Font License) and bundled in brand/fonts/ with their licences, so the app hosts them itself. Fraunces and Manrope are fixed cuts; their variable sources are in fonts/variable/. If one fails to load, headings fall back to Iowan Old Style or Georgia and the rest to the system sans.

Pattern

Ring press

The signature texture. Five strips, each cut from a different slab, inked and pressed so the grain skips the way a real print does.

What it is for

  • Covers: proposals, reports, the first slide.
  • Section openers and the back of a business card.
  • The sign-in screen and the empty workspace on first run.

How to use it

  • Pine on Linen, or tonal: a slightly lighter green on Pine.
  • Crop it. One strip, or part of two, is usually enough.
  • Type sits beside it or in a solid band across it, never directly on the rings.
  • One ring pattern per view.

Two versions

Pressed (PNG) has the dry-brush texture and is for large areas. Carved (SVG) is clean vector for small sizes, print and cutting.

Icons

Icons, cut from one block

{{iconcount}} icons drawn for Nexus, not taken from a library. They are made by the same press as the logo: solid shapes, pressed until every corner is round, with one detail carved out at most. No icon package is installed; the app imports the path data from one generated file.

{{iconsgrid}}

1. Cut, not outlined

Each icon is a solid block of ink. Thin outline icons are what every other product uses; these are not that.

2. One detail

A page has two lines, a card has one stripe, a person has none. If an icon seems to need a second carved detail, it is trying to say two things.

3. Round and quiet

Box shapes keep the soft corner, top right. Growth appears only where it is the meaning: a shoot for a care plan, a leaf for edit, a flower for settings.

Drawing a new icon

  • Work on a 24 unit grid; keep the shape inside 2 to 22.
  • Stroked parts are 2 to 2.8 units thick. One carved detail at most, at least 1.7 units wide, or it closes up at 16 px.
  • Build it from the primitives in brand/tools/build_icons.py and let the press round the corners. Do not hand-edit the output.
  • Check it at 16, 20 and 24 px in both themes before adding it.

In the code

brand/icons/nexus-icons.ts exports every path and a NexusIconName type. An icon is one <svg> with one <path> in currentColor, so it takes the colour of the text beside it. Decorative icons are hidden from screen readers; an icon-only button needs a label.

Individual files are in brand/icons/svg/; a sprite is in brand/icons/nexus-icons.svg.

Nine pieces, plus a moon for night. Scenes are assembled from these, so a new illustration is a new arrangement, not a new style. Files in brand/illustrations/motifs/.

Rules

  • Four colours at most in one scene: an ink, one lighter green, one accent and the ground.
  • The sun or moon is the only place a warm colour appears large.
  • Scenes sit in a frame with the soft corner, on a card surface.
  • No people, faces or hands. Nobody should wonder whether a figure is a patient.

Making a new one

  • Arrange motifs in scenes() in brand/tools/build_patterns.py; give each layer a role, not a colour.
  • Mark anything that belongs only to day or only to night.
  • Keep the horizon in the lower third so the scene crops to a banner.
  • Check it at 104 px wide. If it does not read there, it has too much in it.

Do they move?

No. A scene fades in with its panel over 360 ms and then holds still. Nothing loops, bounces or plays on completion. Motion for buttons and cards is on page {{pg interaction}}.

Limits

  • One illustration per view, never beside a table of live data.
  • Never on an error that needs action, and never in a clinical note.
  • The same scene should not appear twice in one session's worth of screens.

Words

Voice and wording

Nexus talks like a colleague who has done this job for years: friendly, exact, and brief. It says what happened, what it means, and what to do next, and then it stops.

Lines that capture it

"Seven sessions today. Three notes are waiting for your signature."
"Signed. Billing can see it now."
"This claim needs one thing: an active authorization."
"Nothing overdue. Enjoy the quiet."

How it sounds

  • Specific. Counts, names and dates instead of "some items need attention".
  • Short. One idea per sentence. Most messages fit on one line.
  • Warm without performing. A dry aside when things are calm; none when they are not.
  • Steady about hard things. Relapse, discharge and denial are stated plainly, without euphemism or alarm.
  • Honest about limits. If a draft was written by a model, it says so and asks for review.

Before and after

MomentNot thisThis
Empty task listYou have no tasks at this time.Nothing on your list. New tasks from billing and your team land here.
Save failedAn error occurred. Please try again later.That did not save. Your text is still here. Try again, or copy it before you leave the page.
Blocked actionAction unavailable.This note cannot be signed until the session has an end time.

SigningAre you sure you want to sign?Sign this note? After signing, changes are added as amendments.

Words to keep consistent

ClientThe person in care, on every screen. "Patient" only where a payer or form requires it.
SessionA scheduled visit. "Encounter" only in billing and exports.
Note, plan, claimLower case in sentences. No "Progress Note" capitals.
SignThe verb for making a record final. Not "submit", "finalize" or "lock".

Phrases to steer clear of

Healing journey. Empower. Seamless. Unlock. Supercharge. Whoops. Any exclamation mark after an error. They sound like every other product and say less than a plain sentence would.

Numbers and time

$1,202.00, not $1202. Nov 9, not 11/09. 4:12 PM Pacific when the time zone could be in doubt. Durations in minutes: "53 min".

The client and session wording is a recommendation based on how the product reads today; confirm it against payer and state form language before it is treated as settled.

A guide, not a script

  • One idea per slide, in about 25 words or fewer. The facilitator carries the rest.
  • A question slide after each concept. "Any questions?" is not one.
  • Sources and long explanations go in the appendix. A dense slide is a choice with a reason, such as reading an original text.
  • Plain words first. A recovery term is explained the first time it appears.
  • A small Clay leaf in a corner marks "your story goes here" for the facilitator.

Built for a video call

  • Linen background, Heartwood text. Nothing smaller than 28 pt on a 1920 px slide.
  • Keep the top right clear; that is where video tiles sit.

On camera

A baseline above an ordinary call: light on the face from the front, camera at eye level, a separate microphone.

FormWhat it holdsSuggested format

Open decisions and changes

What still needs an owner's answer, what this edition leaves out, and how the guide got here.

Decisions for the owners

  • The line: "Growth, on the record."
  • The wordmark and type: Fraunces and Manrope are new in this edition.
  • The leaf card: which cards get it, and whether the stem stays on every one.
  • The bark line: how wavy it should be.
  • Client and session wording, against payer and state form language.
  • Teaching slides: whether these six layouts are the right set.

Not in this edition

  • A trademark search and recognition testing for the symbol. Both should happen before it goes on anything public.
  • Photography: no images have been chosen or licensed.
  • Editable templates: slides, letterhead, social posts. What is shown are layouts, not files.
  • The executive workspace, charts, calendar and phone layouts.
  • Checkbox and toggle components, and a data-table component. Tables are styled, not yet componentised.
  • A view-by-view review of the restyled app with clinicians and billers.

How it got here

EditionWhat it was
0.1A starter kit: outline icons, three geometric marks, simple patterns. Set aside as too generic.
0.2Rebuilt from the product and the style references: the cross-section symbol, forest-floor palette, ring press and other patterns, cut icons, cut-paper illustration, product screens. Convergence chosen as the symbol on November 11, 2026.
0.3This edition. Bark line on the symbol; new wordmark; Fraunces and Manrope in place of Georgia; the leaf card and its vein; simpler icons; night illustrations; interaction states; teaching materials; contents and index. The unused symbol studies were removed.

Checked in this edition

  • 72 colour pairings, light and dark, against WCAG 2.2 targets: all pass.
  • Every page of this guide reviewed as rendered, in the PDF.

Not checked

  • Contrast on each finished app screen. The tokens are audited; the screens were checked by eye in both themes.
  • The symbol and icons with clinicians or billers.
  • Print: no piece has been proofed on paper.
